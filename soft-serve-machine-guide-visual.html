Four decisions that move the price

each one shifts cost — and pays back differently

1

Feed system

How the mix — and the air — gets into the cylinder

Gravity-fed

Lower cost

Top hopper, gravity feeds it down. Fixed ~40–45% air → denser, richer serve. Fewer parts.

Best for: craft product, froyo by weight, low-maintenance shops

Pump / pressurized

+ moderate · est. $2–5K

A pump pushes mix + air in. Overrun adjustable ~25–100% → lighter, silkier, faster, more consistent.

Best for: high volume, max yield per gallon

VS

Gravity overrun · 40–45%

Pump overrun · 25–100%

BOTTOM LINE Pump makes more product from the same mix — 65% overrun turns 1 gal into 1.65 gal → better margin at volume. Gravity is denser (weighs more if you sell by weight) and cheaper to buy & clean.
2

Cooling

How the machine sheds the heat it makes

Air-cooled

Lower install

Fan across the condenser. Needs ~6" clearance + AC. No plumbing — place it anywhere.

Best for: single machines, food trucks, flexible placement

Water-cooled

+ plumbing · est. $0.5–2K

Water line cools the condenser. Quiet, no clearance, holds up in hot/tight rooms.

Best for: high volume, multiple machines, warm spaces

VS
BOTTOM LINE Air-cooled dumps heat into the room — raising your AC bill, especially with several machines. Water-cooled runs cool & quiet under load; add a glycol chiller to recycle the water.
3

Format

Footprint traded against capacity

Countertop

Entry point

Compact, smaller hopper, ~2–3 servings/min. Sits on a counter, easy for any staff.

Best for: restaurants adding dessert, tight space, moderate volume

Floor model

+ premium · est. $8–20K

Big hopper (20–40 qt), bigger compressor, built for continuous all-day output.

Best for: dedicated shops, high traffic, long lines

VS
BOTTOM LINE Match to throughput: a countertop bottlenecks a busy line; a floor model is expensive dead weight for a few cones an hour. This is the biggest single line-item here.
4

Flavor configuration

How many sellable products from one unit

Single flavor

Simplest build

One cylinder, one flavor. Least to buy, run, and clean.

Best for: one signature flavor, tight budget/space

Twin twist

+ moderate · est. $3–8K

Two flavors + a swirl of both = 3 products from one machine, no extra floor space.

Best for: most mid-volume shops — the twist outsells either single

VS
BOTTOM LINE Twin twist triples the menu at zero added footprint — the default sweet spot. Go single only when space, budget, or a one-flavor concept demands it.

Upgrades that layer on

not either/or — bolt them onto any build

Upgrade · cleaning tech

Heat treatment

WHAT IT ISAn automated nightly pasteurizing cycle keeps the mix safe, so you fully tear down and deep-clean only every 28 days instead of every night.

PRICE IMPACT+ premium — est. $5–12K over a standard model (roughly +30–50%). More complex machine.

TRADE-OFFYou're pre-paying for labor savings — the math rewards steady daily volume. No throughput penalty.

$75–150

saved / machine / month in nightly cleaning labor (at $15/hr)

  • Nightly teardown gone
  • Deep cleans / yr ~13 vs 365
  • Best when staffing is thin

Add-on · flavor injection

Flavor Burst

WHAT IT ISBolts onto your existing spout and injects syrup into and through the serve — up to 8 flavors (255+ combos) from one vanilla cylinder, via a touchscreen. No new freezer, no footprint.

PRICE IMPACTAdd-on, quote-based — commonly ~$8–12K new. Syrup adds only about $0.03 / serving.

TRADE-OFFVendor lift claims are optimistic — results ride on foot traffic + upsell discipline. Adds a daily rinse and monthly strip-down.

+$0.75–1.50

upcharge / flavored serving on a ~$0.03 cost

  • Claimed sales lift 100–300%
  • Worked examples +$14–22K/yr
  • Payback (busy) < 6 mo

Quick read, by concept

Craft / by-weight

Gravity-fed, countertop or twin — denser serve weighs more, cleans up simple

High-volume shop

Pump + floor + twin, water-cooled, heat treatment if late-night cleaning is a risk

Variety on a budget

Keep the base simple, bolt on Flavor Burst — many flavors, one cylinder
