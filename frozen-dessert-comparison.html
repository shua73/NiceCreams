How it's made:
ice cream, soft serve & froyo

The same five components — ice, protein, fat, sugar, air — tuned three different ways. Fat and air do most of the work; the texture agents and the freezing method decide the rest, including whether it can survive in a pint.

Traditional

Ice cream

Dairy cream base, hard-frozen and stored cold

Served fresh

Soft serve

Low-fat mix, whipped and dispensed on the spot

Cultured

Frozen yogurt

Yogurt base, tangy — served soft or packed

Core process & machinery

how it gets frozen
Make itmix → freeze → (harden)
Mix, pasteurize, homogenize, then age 4–24 h. Churn in a batch or continuous freezer (whips air in), draw soft at ~−5 °C, then harden to −18 °C.
Liquid mix goes straight into the machine and is dispensed immediately at ~−4 to −6 °C. No aging, no hardening step.
Culture the yogurt (or start from ready-made), blend the mix, then either run it through a soft-serve machine (shops) or batch-freeze + harden (pints).
Machinery
Batch or continuous freezer + a blast / hardening freezer for storage.
Soft-serve machine — a chilled cylinder with a scraper blade and a built-in air pump.
Soft-serve machine for shops, or batch freezer + hardening freezer for packed pints.

Packability

can it be sold in a pint?
Pint viabilitysurvives freezer storage & heat-shock?
Yes — built for it Designed to be hardened and stored. The fat/sugar/stabilizer balance resists re-crystallizing through storage and temperature swings. Add: a stabilizer blend + emulsifier for a clean scoop.
Not as-is Meant to be eaten fresh and soft. Freeze it hard for a pint and it loses the signature texture. To make it work you essentially reformulate toward low-fat ice cream — more sugar, solids, and stabilizer.
Yes — with tweaks Add: extra sugar or corn-syrup solids (lower the freezing point so it scoops and to offset tang), stabilizers for storage, often an emulsifier. Note the live cultures fade in the freezer.
05

Texture agents

Stabilizers bind water and tame ice crystals; emulsifiers disperse fat and pull in air. The trick: strong emulsifiers act as destabilizers — they partially coalesce the fat, which is what gives soft serve its dry, stand-up-peak texture. Chips show where each one typically shows up.

Emulsifiers & destabilizers — disperse fat, pull in air, build dryness

Mono- & diglyceride flakes

The commercial emulsifier. Drives fat destabilization → dry, stiff texture and better overrun.

Polysorbate 80
